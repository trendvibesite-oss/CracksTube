1. Mercury: First Best Friends Position

Mercury is the first planet in the Snapchat planets order. Seeing your Bitmoji represented by Mercury means you occupy the first position in the relevant person's Best Friends list.

The Mercury design is commonly shown as a reddish planet surrounded by red hearts. This makes it one of the most recognizable symbols in the Friend Solar System.

However, the position reflects Snapchat activity. It does not prove that someone considers you their closest friend in every aspect of life.

2. Venus: Second Position

Venus represents the second position in the Best Friends list. Its visual design commonly features a light-colored planet with colorful hearts.

If your Bitmoji appears on Venus, it indicates that you occupy the second position in that person's solar system.

It is important not to confuse the position with a fixed friendship label. Your ranking may differ depending on the account being viewed.

3. Earth: Third Position

Earth represents the third position in the planetary sequence. Its familiar blue-and-green appearance, often accompanied by a Moon and other visual details, distinguishes it from the surrounding planets.

A third-place position means that you occupy the third position in the relevant Best Friends list. It does not reveal a numerical interaction score or explain exactly how much activity separates you from the person ranked second.

4. Mars: Fourth Position

Mars represents the fourth position. Its reddish appearance and surrounding decorative elements make it visually distinct.

If you appear as Mars in someone's Friend Solar System, you are in the fourth position in that person's list.

The symbol should be understood as a ranking indicator rather than evidence that a friendship is becoming weaker or stronger outside Snapchat.

5. Jupiter: Fifth Position

Jupiter is the fifth planet in the Snapchat planets order. It is generally represented by a large, orange-toned planet.

A Jupiter position means that you occupy the fifth place in the relevant Best Friends list. It does not automatically mean that the friendship is casual or unimportant.

The amount and type of interaction displayed by Snapchat cannot fully capture the context of a personal relationship.

6. Saturn: Sixth Position

Saturn represents the sixth position and is recognizable by its prominent rings.

If your Bitmoji appears on Saturn, you occupy the sixth position in that person's Best Friends list.

The planet's visual design is inspired by the real Saturn, but its meaning within Snapchat is determined by the feature's ranking system rather than the astronomical properties of the planet.

7. Uranus: Seventh Position

Uranus represents the seventh position. Its appearance is generally associated with a blue-green or green-colored planet.

Seeing Uranus means that you occupy the seventh position in the relevant solar system.

This does not mean that a person dislikes you or that your relationship has no value. The feature reflects a limited set of activity-based information, not every interaction that matters between two people.

8. Neptune: Eighth Position

Neptune is the eighth and final planet in the Friend Solar System sequence. It is commonly depicted as a blue planet.

If your Bitmoji appears on Neptune, you occupy the eighth position in that person's Best Friends list.

Although Neptune is the farthest planet in this system, it should not be interpreted as proof that the friendship is unimportant. People communicate through different channels, and Snapchat activity is only one part of a relationship.

How Does the Friend Solar System Work?

The Friend Solar System is a Snapchat+ feature that represents positions in a user's Best Friends list through planetary symbols.

Snapchat identifies Best Friends as people with whom a user Snaps and Chats most frequently. The feature uses the eight positions represented by Mercury through Neptune to provide a visual indication of where a friend appears in the list.

The system is account-specific. Your position in one person's solar system may not be the same as their position in yours.

For example, you might appear as Mercury when viewing one friend's profile but appear as Jupiter when checking another person's solar system. That difference reflects the rankings associated with each account.

Snapchat does not publicly provide a precise formula showing the weight assigned to every type of interaction. Therefore, claims about exact message counts, percentages, or guaranteed ranking changes should not be treated as confirmed rules. How to Enable Snapchat Planets

The Friend Solar System is available to Snapchat+ subscribers, but Snapchat says the feature is turned off by default for first-time subscribers. You may need to enable it manually.

Follow these general steps:

  1. Open Snapchat on your mobile device.
  2. Tap your profile or Bitmoji icon.
  3. Open the Snapchat+ membership section.
  4. Find the Friend Solar System or Solar System setting in feature management.
  5. Switch the feature on.
  6. Open a friend's profile and visit their Friendship Profile.
  7. Look for the relevant Best Friends or Friends badge.
  8. Tap the badge to view the planet, if the feature is available for that account.

The interface may change as Snapchat updates the app, so menu labels and placement can vary.

If you cannot find the setting, confirm that your Snapchat+ subscription is active and check the official support instructions for the current interface.

Can You Use Snapchat Planets Without Snapchat+?

The Friend Solar System is a Snapchat+ feature. Without the required subscription access, you should not expect to see the full planet-ranking experience.

Be cautious of third-party websites or applications that promise to unlock Snapchat planets for free. An informational article can explain the feature, but it cannot legitimately change your account's subscription permissions.

Never share your password, authentication codes, or account recovery details with a site claiming to reveal hidden planet rankings.
