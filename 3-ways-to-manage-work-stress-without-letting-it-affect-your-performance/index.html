3 Ways to Manage Work Stress Without Letting It Affect Your Performance

Figure 1: Work-related stress, long hours, and heavy workloads impacting employee wellbeing and mental health.
Table of contents
  • Change What You Do Before and After Work to Reduce Stress
  • Change How You Prioritise Work
  • Introduce Daily Workplace Stress-Reducing Strategies

According to official Health and Safety Executive (HSE) data, 964,000 UK workers suffer from work-related stress, depression, or anxiety. CIPHR also found that 70% to 79% of UK employees find their jobs stressful. In fact, there are many studies and data we could discuss relating to workplace stress.

Whether it's due to performance pressure overload or the environment itself being stress-inducing, like a hospital, going into work knowing you'll face stress can induce anxiety before the day has even started.

Here are three ways to manage that stress and make the workplace more enjoyable. Just as organizations prevent systemic burnout by establishing sustainable operational workflows outlined in our ai transformation is a problem of governance executive leadership analysis, individuals must take proactive command over their daily routines to safeguard their mental wellbeing.

Change What You Do Before and After Work to Reduce Stress

If you wake up already dreading going into work, start by changing what you do before your 9-5 to create a calming presence. Some people prefer to take a holistic approach with supplements such as ashwagandha or magnesium, and others believe good quality CBD, as you can find on highnsupply.co.uk, can be helpful.

Or it can be a routine you develop that helps you feel at peace before you leave the house. For example, a slow coffee, a nice shower and morning routine, a 30-minute walk, or some time in the gym.

What you definitely shouldn't do is snooze your alarm, wake up with minutes to spare before leaving the house, and leave feeling stressed.

Figure 2: Creating a mindful morning routine with fresh coffee and daily planning before starting work.

Change How You Prioritise Work

Do you have a structure to your working day, or do you just switch between tasks and hope for the best?

Yes, it's sometimes difficult to structure a working day if every day is different, but you can prioritise work based on how much energy you have and how much energy a task requires. Try to get the most difficult task out of the way when you have enough energy, and stop treating every task as an emergency. Much like categorizing priority tiers and relationship indicators explained in our snapchatplanetsorders com website guide and mobile ranking feature breakdown, ranking your daily responsibilities by urgency and importance brings structured order to an otherwise overwhelming task list.

You could also try the Eisenhower Matrix, which divides tasks into four sections:

  • Do right away
  • Schedule for later
  • Give to someone else if possible
  • Delete completely if not needed

Introduce Daily Workplace Stress-Reducing Strategies

This section might technically give you more than one way to reduce stress. Think of it as a holistic approach: make small changes to how your day flows, not just what work you do and when.

One big reason people have more stress than they should is that they often lack clear boundaries at work. Employees often work through lunch breaks, start early and finish late, and take on more work than is required. So the first step is setting boundaries, such as starting and finishing on time, unless there's an occasional exception and you don't mind. Similar to maintaining steady progress and disciplined milestone pacing explored in our tycoon club mobile loyalty rewards and gamified perks breakdown, recognizing personal effort while respecting sustainable working limits ensures high performance without risking emotional exhaustion.

Figure 3: Fostering a supportive, positive work culture with clear boundaries and manageable workloads.

Then consider taking 5-minute microbreaks after 25 minutes of work (the Pomodoro Technique). This one is technically harder if you work in the office and can be seen as slacking if you move away from your desk every 25 minutes. At home, it's much easier.

Instead of taking a break to go on your phone or go for a walk, take 5 minutes to make some slow notes or make a drink. Alternate between breaks that involve moving away from your workstation and ones that are less mentally taxing but still look like work.

The Health and Safety Executive (HSE) suggests taking frequent, short breaks, such as 5 to 10 minutes every hour, but as it's not a legal requirement, some workplaces can frown upon it unless they're done strategically.

Workplace stress doesn't have to be as intense as it feels. Some workplaces are naturally more stress-inducing than others, but plenty of small changes can bring stress levels down and make going into work more enjoyable.
