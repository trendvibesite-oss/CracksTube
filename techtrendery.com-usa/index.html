Does .com Mean a Website Is American?

No.

This is a common misconception.

.com is a generic top-level domain used internationally.

A company operating from India, Canada, Germany, Australia, Japan, Brazil, or virtually anywhere else can use a .com domain.

Similarly, a website can:

  • Use U.S. hosting
  • Have American visitors
  • Discuss American companies
  • Cover U.S. technology
  • Rank in Google USA
  • Use U.S.-based infrastructure

without being headquartered in the United States.

Therefore, server location, audience location, and corporate location should not be treated as the same thing.

How to Evaluate a Technology Website

When you find a technology article through Google, don't judge it only by its design or ranking position.

Use a simple framework.

1. Identify the Author

Look for a real byline and relevant expertise.

For a simple smartphone guide, extensive professional credentials may not be necessary.

For cybersecurity or health technology, expertise becomes much more important.

2. Check the Date

Technology ages quickly.

An article about the "best AI tools" from two years ago may already be substantially outdated.

3. Find Primary Sources

If an article says a company launched a new feature, check the company's documentation or announcement.

If it discusses research, look for the original study.

4. Separate Facts From Opinions

Statements such as:

"The software costs $20 per month"

can be verified.

Statements such as:

"This is the best software for everyone"

are subjective.

5. Check Regional Relevance

For techtrendery.com usa searches, this step is particularly important.

Make sure pricing, regulations, services, and product availability apply to U.S. users.
