It can tell you how often a phrase appears.

What Is Keyword Frequency?

Keyword frequency simply means how many times a particular keyword or phrase appears in a piece of content.

Figure 2: Tracking keyword frequency during editing ensures primary search terms appear naturally without sounding mechanical or repetitive.

For example:

Article length: 1,500 words

Target keyword:

keyword research tool

Occurrences:

8

Keyword frequency:

8

This is one of the easiest SEO metrics to understand.

It can help writers discover accidental underuse or obvious repetition.

However, frequency alone doesn't tell you whether those appearances are useful.

An article could mention a keyword 20 times and still provide a poor answer.

Another article could mention it only several times while comprehensively covering the subject.

Context matters.

What Is Keyword Density?

Keyword density expresses keyword frequency as a percentage of the total word count.

The basic formula is:

Keyword Density = (Keyword Occurrences ÷ Total Words) × 100

For example:

Article Word Count Keyword Uses Approx. Density
1,000 5 0.5%
1,000 10 1%
1,500 15 1%
2,000 20 1%
2,500 25 1%

These figures are mathematical examples, not recommended Google targets.

That distinction is important.

Is There a Perfect Keyword Density for Google?

No.

There is no official Google rule saying that every SEO article should contain a keyword at a particular percentage.

A common mistake among inexperienced SEO writers is to treat keyword density as if it were a ranking score.

For example:

"My competitor has 1.4%, so I need 1.5%."

That approach can quickly produce unnatural writing.

Google's spam guidance specifically identifies keyword stuffing as problematic when keywords are loaded onto a page in an attempt to manipulate rankings, particularly when repetition becomes unnatural.

The better approach is:

Use the keyword where it genuinely helps the reader understand the page.

This is the most important question.

Does Word Count Matter for SEO?

Word count is useful for understanding the size of an article, but longer content isn't automatically better.

A 5,000-word article can be worse than a 1,200-word article if the shorter page answers the question more effectively.

Don't add 2,000 words simply because another page has 2,000 words.

Instead, ask:

What information does the reader need to make a decision or solve the problem?

Then cover that information thoroughly.

Does Keyword Density Affect Google Rankings?

Keyword usage can help communicate the subject of a page, but keyword density should not be treated as an independent ranking formula.

Modern search systems evaluate pages using many signals and can understand topics through language, context and relationships between concepts.

Google's guidance also warns against keyword stuffing and emphasizes useful, people-first content.

So the practical SEO approach is:

Use relevant keywords naturally, then focus on satisfying the searcher's intent.
